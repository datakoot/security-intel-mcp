# KEV-addition forecast pilot (proposal)

A small, pre-registered experiment: can forecasters beat EPSS at predicting which CVEs CISA will add to the
Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog within 30 days?

**User and decision.** An engineer triaging vulnerability alerts: patch now, or wait for the next cycle?
A KEV listing is what makes a CVE urgent, so the forecast target is catalog addition, not exploitation.

**Question (binary, Brier).** "Will <CVE> be added to the CISA KEV catalog on or before <close> (UTC, by `dateAdded`)?"
Settles YES if and only if the CVE appears in the KEV feed with `dateAdded` <= close (first published value).
Evidence: the [KEV JSON](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json) and
[CSV](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/csv/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.csv) feeds.

**Candidates (frozen before the window opens).** The 10 highest-[EPSS](https://www.first.org/epss/) CVEs with a
current-year ID that are not yet in KEV. Generated by [`kev_candidates.py`](kev_candidates.py) (stdlib Python,
public feeds only).

**Baselines.** (1) Each CVE's EPSS score at open (FIRST: probability of exploitation activity in the next 30 days);
(2) the historical base rate; (3) 0.5.

**Plan.** A one-week dry run of 10 questions, then 4 weeks of 10 questions each. Analysis: mean Brier per
participant against the three baselines, plus a reliability plot.

Week 1 (proposed): [`week1-2026-10-15.json`](week1-2026-10-15.json), window 2026-10-15 to 2026-11-14,
KEV catalog 2026.10.14 at open.
